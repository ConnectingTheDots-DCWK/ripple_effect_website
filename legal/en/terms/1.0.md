## B10. Deleting your account

You may delete your account at any time, from the application or by writing to

### B10.1 The thirty days

Unless you choose otherwise, deletion is **reversible for thirty (30) days**.
In that window:

- the account is closed — nobody can sign in to it, and it stops appearing to

- **you can restore it**, by signing in with the same credentials, and
  everything comes back as it was;
- **you can export** all of Your Content, in a form the application can open;
- nothing is deleted, and nothing is billed.

The window exists because account deletion is the one action in this service
that cannot be undone, and people click it by accident, in anger, and on the
wrong account.

### B10.2 Deleting immediately instead

The deletion screen offers a **checkbox to skip the thirty days**. Tick it and
erasure begins at once, there is no restore, and we cannot recover anything
afterwards — so export first if you want a copy.

**One honest limit, and it applies to both routes.** "Immediately" means from
our live systems. Backups are written on a cycle and are not rewritten to
remove individual records; they expire and are destroyed on their own
schedule, within a maximum of **one hundred and eighty (180) days**. Until
then a copy of your data exists in a backup that nobody reads and that is
restored only if a disaster requires it. Anybody who tells you a backup can be
surgically erased on request is describing a system they do not run.

### B10.3 What is erased

Everything we hold that identifies you or belongs to you:

- your account — e-mail address, credentials, settings, sessions;
- **workspaces you own**, and all their content, media and history;
- your membership of every workspace, and the invitations behind it;
- collaboration and presence records;
- support correspondence, unless a dispute is open between us.

### B10.4 What is not, and why

Two things survive, and both are deliberately small.

**Content in a workspace you did not own.** If you contributed to somebody
else's workspace, what you contributed stays with that workspace — deleting
your account cannot reach into another person's project and take pieces out of
it, any more than leaving a job deletes the documents you wrote there. **Your
identity is dissociated from it**: your name and address are removed and your
contributions are attributed to a deleted account. If you want something you
wrote removed from a workspace you do not own, ask its owner; if you believe
it is there unlawfully, write to <legal@ripplefx.app> and we will
deal with it.

**A minimal record that an account accepted these Terms** — which document,
which version, and when, with a one-way hash of the account's e-mail address
in place of the address itself — kept for as long as a claim under them could
be brought, and used for nothing else. It cannot be used to contact you or to
rebuild any part of your account, and it can be matched to you only by
someone who already knows your address. It is kept
under Article 17(3)(e) GDPR, which allows retention for the establishment,
exercise or defence of legal claims, and it exists so that neither of us has
to rely on memory about what was agreed. Anything the law obliges us to keep
for tax or accounting is kept too, for the period the law names and no longer.

### B10.5 Workspaces you own, and the people in them

If you delete an account that owns a workspace with other members in it, **we
tell them before anything is erased** and give them the same thirty days to
export their work — that period runs from our notice, not from your request,

You may instead **transfer the workspace to another member** before deleting,
