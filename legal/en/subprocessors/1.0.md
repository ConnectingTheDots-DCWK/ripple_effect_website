**Ripple Effect — Sub-processors, version 1.0 (English)**
In force as published, and kept current as the list changes.

The third parties that process personal data on our behalf, what each one is
for, and where it does it.

This list exists separately from the [Privacy Notice](/privacy/) on purpose:
changing a supplier should not require reissuing a notice, and a notice that
has to be reissued to correct a supplier's name is a notice that stays wrong
for a while. The Privacy Notice points here, and here is where the list is
kept current.

**Polar is not our processor.** It is the seller of record and decides, as a
controller in its own right, what it does with the billing details you give
it. We receive reports and payouts rather than your card details, and for tax
purposes we invoice Polar rather than you. Its own privacy notice governs that
data.

**Forward Email is a forwarder, not a mailbox.** Its own privacy policy says
it does not write forwarded messages to disk or to a database and keeps no
logs or IP addresses for them; what it does is hand the message on to us,
after which it sits in our own mailbox like any other e-mail. It does not
publish where its servers are, which is why the cell above says so rather
than guessing, and it is a United States company — its terms are governed by
the law of Delaware — so the transfer basis below is the one that applies to
it.

**There is no CAPTCHA provider.** Sign-up is protected by a proof-of-work
check that your device computes and our own server verifies, so no third
party sees anything for it.

**An AI provider is named here before a feature that uses one ships**, never
after, and we will not offer a feature from a provider that does not
contractually agree that what is sent is not used to train its models.

## Transfers outside the European Union

Where a provider processes data outside the European Economic Area, the
transfer is made under Chapter V GDPR — but the basis is not the same one for
all of them, and saying so matters more than keeping the sentence short.

- **The United Kingdom**, where our Supabase project runs, is covered by the
  European Commission's **adequacy decisions of 19 December 2025**, which
  renewed the 2021 decisions until **27 December 2031**. A transfer to a
  country the Commission has found adequate needs no further safeguard under
  article 45. Were that adequacy withdrawn, suspended or allowed to lapse, the
  transfer would fall back on the Standard Contractual Clauses in our
  data-processing agreement with the provider, which is why one is in place
  regardless.
- **The United States** — Vercel, GitHub, Resend, Polar, Forward Email — is
  covered for a given provider either by the **EU–US Data Privacy Framework**,
  the Commission's adequacy decision of 10 July 2023, where that provider is
  certified under it, or otherwise by the Commission's **Standard Contractual
  Clauses** under article 46, together with whatever supplementary measures
  our data-processing agreement with it sets out.
- **The European Union** is not a transfer. Tally processes in Belgium, and
  nothing leaves the Union for it.

## Changes to this list

**We will give thirty (30) days' notice before a new sub-processor starts
processing personal data on our behalf**, by updating this page and — for
accountholders — by e-mail. A business customer under a data-processing
agreement may object to a new sub-processor within that period on reasonable
data-protection grounds; if we cannot resolve the objection, that customer may
terminate the affected part of the service and receive a pro-rata refund of
the unused prepaid period.

Replacing a provider with another that does the same job under equivalent
safeguards is treated the same way. Removing one is not, and takes effect when
it happens.

**This list is kept current rather than versioned on a schedule**, and the
notice period above is what protects you instead. It is a list of facts, not a
set of terms: there is nothing here to accept, nothing that could be in force
for one reader and not another, and no date on which it starts applying. It is
in force as published. That is the whole reason it is a separate document from
the Privacy Notice — a notice that had to be reissued to correct a supplier's
name is a notice that stays wrong for a while.

Every version of this list that has ever been in force stays published at its
