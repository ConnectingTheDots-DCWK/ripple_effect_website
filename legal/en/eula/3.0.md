## 7. What the application sends, and when

**From version 1.5, the application makes no network request of any kind until
you have accepted this Agreement.** There is no exception to that — not for
updates, not for diagnostics, not for anything else. Nothing is sent before
you have agreed that anything may be. The acceptance step is not a notice
drawn over a running application: until you answer it, the application beneath
it has not been built, so there is nothing there to make a request.

A build earlier than 1.5 does not ask for acceptance and makes the update
check described in rule 3 below on first launch; that is the only request it
makes unasked, and it carries nothing about you. The engine-fix check and the
usage statistics both arrive with 1.5, so neither exists in a build that does
not ask.

Once you have accepted, what leaves your machine is governed by **four
rules**. They are the terms. The [Privacy Notice](/privacy/), §4, is the
complete list of the requests the application makes under them — what each
one carries, when it is made, and how long anything is kept — and it says
each new one before the release that starts making it.

1. **Nothing you author, and nothing that identifies you, leaves without your
   say.** Your passages, boards, projects, scripts and exports are not sent
   anywhere by the application. An identifier — of you, of your installation
   or of your machine — is sent only as part of something you switched on or
   answered yes to under rule 2.
2. **Anything about you is yours to switch on, or to answer.**
   - **Usage statistics** are off until you switch them on. The application
     asks once, with the complete list of what would be sent in front of the
     question and neither answer preselected; the setting can be switched off
     at any time, and switching it off stops them at once. No content you
     author is ever included.
   - **A crash report** is sent only if you answer yes to that report. After a
     crash the next launch names every file the report holds and says what
     each one contains. There is no setting that answers in advance, and
     declining is final for that report. Before asking, the application may
     check that the server it would send to can be reached, with a request
     that carries nothing about you.
   - Anything else of this kind is added to this list, which is a new version
     of this Agreement.
3. **What the application fetches for itself carries nothing about you, and
   is asked only of our own site.** The application may ask `ripplefx.app`
   or one of its subdomains for what it shows you or needs in order to keep
   working: whether a newer release exists, whether a fix exists for its
   engine, the news, the pictures that go with them, the people credited in
   the About box. Such a request carries **no identifier and no information
   about you**. Where it carries anything at all, it is a fact about the copy
   of the Software — its version, its platform, the engine it runs — and the
   Privacy Notice names it. Where a comparison has to be made, such as
   whether a release is newer than yours, it is made on your machine. A fix
   to the engine is downloaded, checked against our signature and used from
   the next launch, and it never changes what the Software can do.
   **Whatever of this kind the application does on its own, rather than
   because you opened something, can be switched off in its settings** —
   except the check for a newer release, which carries nothing at all.
4. **Anybody else is reached only where this rule names them, or because you
   sent something yourself.**
   - **GitHub**, for two things. The pictures of the people credited in the
     About box are fetched from GitHub by your own machine rather than
     through our site, and one such address is compiled into the application
     and is requested even when the list itself cannot be fetched. And a fix
     to the engine, or an installer, is downloaded from GitHub through a
     redirect on our site. Nothing identifying you goes with either.
   - **The Service, if you sign in.** Signing in, and anything you then do
     with the hosted features, goes to our servers under the [Terms of
     Service](/terms/). Nothing is sent for the Service before you sign in.
   - **Whatever you send yourself.** Version control pushes to a repository
     you configured, with credentials you supplied, held in your operating
     system's credential store. Opening documentation or a support page hands
     a URL to your browser, through a redirect on `ripplefx.app` that sees
     that request and nothing else. And a document in a project may point at
     a picture by `http` or `https` address rather than at a file in the
     folder, in which case drawing that document fetches it from whoever
     hosts it — **including in a project somebody else sent you**, whose
     documents you did not write. We are not a party to anything beyond it.
   - Adding anybody to this rule is a new version of this Agreement.

The Software keeps a session log on your machine. It is never sent on its own —
there is no uploader for it — and the only way it leaves your machine is
inside a crash report you choose to send under rule 2, whose question names it
among the files the report holds before you answer. A session that ended
without anything going wrong deletes its log automatically.

What each request involves, what a crash report contains and how long
anything is kept is in the [Privacy Notice](/privacy/), which forms part of

## 14. Updates and changes to this Agreement

Updates to the Software — including fixes to its engine that the application
downloads itself (§7) — are governed by this Agreement unless they are
accompanied by different terms.

This Agreement is versioned `major.minor`, and the two halves mean different
things:

- A **minor** version changes the wording without changing the terms —
  a correction, a clarification, a better sentence. It is published, applies
  immediately, and you are not asked again.
- A **major** version changes the terms. You are notified in the application
  and on the website, and asked to accept it **no sooner than thirty (30)
  days** after that notice. Until you accept it, the version you did accept is
  the one that governs you.

**This Agreement attaches to the software, not to a date.** It takes effect for
your installation when you accept it, and a version of it is therefore in
force for you and not for somebody who has not. That is why no article here
names a day of the month: the only moment that matters to you is the one at
which you were asked.

**A new feature is not a new version.** "The Software" in Article 1 means
Ripple Effect as we distribute it, *including every component shipped with
it* — so a part of the application that did not exist when you accepted this
Agreement is governed by it without this Agreement changing. What makes a new
version is a change to what this Agreement **says**: what you may do, what we
owe you, what either of us is liable for. Not a change to what the application
does. And nothing reaches backwards: a later version never governs your use of
the Software before you accepted it.

**A version that is not yet in force may be corrected.** Until a version has
been accepted by somebody, or published as final, it binds nobody and is
evidence of nothing — so a mistake in it is corrected where it stands rather
than by issuing a version to fix a comma. Once either of those has happened,
the text at its permanent address never changes again, and any change is a new
version under the procedure above.

**That is not a right to amend terms you have accepted**, and it is worth
saying so plainly. The version you accepted stays exactly as you read it, at
its own address, for as long as the record is worth anything. Nothing in this
article lets us alter it.

Every version that has ever been in force stays published at its own permanent
address, so the version you accepted remains readable. This one is at
<https://legal.ripplefx.app/v/eula-3.0/>.

If you do not accept a major version, you may continue to use the last build
of the Software released under the version you did accept, under that version;
you simply may not use builds released under the new one. If you had paid for
